Chapter 1

What ExactPoint is

Your system is one product with three doors:

  • The website — exactpointrepairs.com. Customers find you, read about your services, see your real work, and book appointments online — day or night, without calling.
  • The Service Manager — exactpointrepairs.com/manager. Your office. Only you can enter it. Every request, customer, appointment, technician, quote, and invoice lives here.
  • The technician app — exactpointrepairs.com/tech. What you and any technician use in the field, on a phone: today's jobs, navigate, call the customer, take photos, mark the job done.
The one promise the system always keeps: no technician can ever be booked in two places at the same time. Not by a customer, not by you by accident, not by two customers clicking at the same moment. The database itself refuses. Two different technicians at the same time is fine — that's a busy day, not a conflict.

Chapter 2

Signing in & your password

  1. Go to exactpointrepairs.com and scroll to the bottom. Tap Team Sign-in.
  2. Email: info@exactpointrepairs.com. Password: the one you set. You land on your Manager dashboard.
  3. Forgot it? Tap Forgot password? on the sign-in page. A reset link comes to your email within a few minutes (it expires after 1 hour).
  4. To change your password anytime: Manager → Settings → Password.

Technicians sign in the same way with their own accounts and see only their own jobs — never your full customer list, money, or settings. Emails sent to info@exactpointrepairs.com are forwarded to your inbox automatically.

This handbook is always one tap away inside the app: Help in your Manager menu, or the help link on the technician screen. It's kept up to date as the system grows.

Chapter 3

What your customer sees

Knowing their side helps you trust yours. A customer who books online:

  1. Picks the appliance or HVAC problem and describes it — they can add photos of the machine and its model sticker.
  2. Enters their address. The system checks it against Google's records, finds the exact rooftop, and quietly works out which part of Houston it's in.
  3. Sees appointment windows that are real — the system already checked who on your team has the skill, the working hours, and enough driving time to get there. Customers never see technician names.
  4. Must tick a box acknowledging your $80 diagnostic fee policy before submitting. It's in writing, every time.
  5. Gets a reference number like EP-1052 on screen and a confirmation email. Their chosen window is held for them, pending your confirmation.

On your side, that booking appears instantly in Requests and on your Today screen.

Chapter 4

Your morning: the Today screen

Open /manager with your coffee. Top row of numbers:

  • Appointments — today's booked visits.
  • In progress — jobs where someone is en route, arrived, or working right now.
  • Techs working — how many people have jobs today.
  • New requests — bookings waiting for you to schedule. Orange means "look at me."
  • Emergencies — customers who marked their request urgent.
  • Needs reassignment — jobs whose technician was deactivated, or that lost their assignment.

Below that, Needs attention lists each new request with a Schedule → shortcut, then Today's schedule shows every visit in time order with its status. This screen is your control tower — if it's calm, your day is handled.

Finding anything, fast: the search box at the top right of every Manager screen finds customers (name, phone, or email), requests (type the EP-number), and invoices (type the INV-number). One box, straight to the record.

Chapter 5

Booking the work

A. A request came in from the website

  1. Open Requests (or tap it from Today). New ones are under the new tab.
  2. Open the request. You'll see the problem, any photos the customer sent, their contact, the address with its zone, and the windows they preferred. If this customer has an active warranty with you, a bright Possible warranty callback banner appears — decide yourself whether the new issue is the same repair.
  3. Look at Recommended. The system ranks technician + time options and tells you why each one — "2 existing jobs in the same zone that day", "≈12 min from the nearest job", "adds ≈31 min of driving". Higher score = less windshield time and a tighter day.
  4. Tap Assign on the option you like. Done — the appointment is booked, the customer gets a confirmation email, and it appears on the Calendar. The request page keeps an Open the job → button afterwards, taking you straight to the job's page.
  5. Don't like the suggestions? Use Manual override below them: pick any technician and time yourself. The system still refuses impossible choices (wrong skill, double-booked, not enough driving time) but otherwise trusts you — and records that it was your call.
  6. Junk or a duplicate? Decline or Spam.

B. A customer called you on the phone

  1. Manager → ＋ New Booking.
  2. Type their phone number first. If they've used you before, their name and addresses appear — tap to reuse them. History stays connected.
  3. Fill in the service, the problem in their words, and the address (it gets verified the same way as online bookings). Tick urgent if it is.
  4. Mention the $80 diagnostic fee on the call — the form records that you did.
  5. Tap Create booking → pick a time. You land straight on the recommendations screen — assign exactly as above, while they're still on the line if you like.

Customer details are editable: open Customers → the person → Edit details to fix a name, phone, email, or add notes. Addresses aren't edited freehand (they're verified and mapped) — a new address gets added the next time you book them.

The $80 rule, in one line: the diagnostic/service-call fee is owed only when you diagnose and the customer declines the repair. Approve the quote and the fee is waived. The system applies this automatically when you invoice (Chapter 9).

Chapter 6

The Calendar

Manager → Calendar. Each technician gets a column ("lane") in their own color, 7 AM to 7 PM. Every block is a job; gray hatched areas are time off or blocked time — protected, nothing can be booked there.

  • Open a job: click its block.
  • Move a job (same day): drag its block to another time or another technician's lane. If the move would double-book someone or the drive is impossible, it bounces back with the reason.
  • Move a job to another day: open the job → Reschedule → pick the new date/time (and technician if you want). Call the customer first — the system moves the window with the job.
  • Block time: the Block time button — pick one technician or the whole company, a start and end, and a reason (supplier run, doctor, holiday). Blocked time disappears from what customers can book immediately.
  • Week view: one tap switches to a 7-day grid — jobs per technician per day, with the zones they're in. Great for spotting a lopsided week.

Chapter 7

Routes & the Map

Routes shows each technician's day as a stop list with real Houston driving times between stops. When reordering the visits would save meaningful driving, an Optimize Day suggestion appears with the minutes saved — tap Accept optimization to make that the plan. It never changes the time windows customers were promised; it only changes visit order.

Map puts the day on a Houston map — numbered pins in each technician's color, connected in visit order. Tap a pin for the job; the unassigned list sits underneath. Area View shows the coming week as jobs-per-zone-per-day — scattered single jobs across town are money burned on gas, and this view makes them visible.

Chapter 8

Running a job (the technician app)

On the phone, /tech shows today's jobs in order. Opening one gives two big buttons — Call customer and Navigate — plus the problem description and any customer photos. The workflow is four taps across the visit:

  1. En route when driving there.
  2. Arrived at the door.
  3. Start when work begins. Add notes ("drain pump clogged, cleared and tested") and photos — before, the model/serial sticker, after. These live with the job forever.
  4. Complete job… — write what was repaired. This one matters: it closes the job and automatically starts the 2-month labor warranty on that specific repair. The customer gets a completion email.

You see every status change live in the Manager. Technicians can only open jobs assigned to them.

Chapter 9

Money: quotes & invoices

Two ways in: open any job and find Quote & invoice — or, for a customer who wants a price before booking a visit, open their page (Customers or search) and tap Create quote. Either way, the same builder:

  1. Add lines — a part, labor, anything — with quantity and price. Subtotal, tax (8.25% unless you change it), and total calculate live. Save quote. Changed your mind on a price? Edit and tap Update quote — every save is a new revision (the label at the top shows which one you're on), and the earlier ones stay readable under Quote history. Re-send after a change so the customer has the latest.
  2. Tap Email quote to customer to send it — with the fee-waiver promise spelled out — or present it in person. A status chip appears under the buttons and updates on its own: Sending…, then Sent — awaiting delivery confirmation, then Delivered once their mail server has accepted it (usually within a minute). Bounced means the address rejected it — fix the email on the customer's page, then tap Resend quote. The button is safe to tap more than once: it never sends the same quote twice within a minute. Then record their answer: Customer approved or Customer declined.
  3. The invoice writes itself: approved → quote + tax, $80 fee waived. Declined → an invoice for exactly the $80 diagnostic fee. Every invoice gets a number like INV-1017.
  4. To collect: open the invoice number and tap Email invoice to customer — they receive the full breakdown with the total due and your payment methods. Tap Print / Save PDF on the same page for a paper or PDF copy with your logo.
  5. When money comes in, tap Record payment (on the job's Quote & invoice panel or on the invoice itself). The amount is prefilled with the balance due — change it for a deposit or a partial payment. Pick Zelle / Cash / Check, add the check number or Zelle confirmation if you have it, and Save payment. Each payment gets its own receipt number (RCT-1001…), a receipt email goes to the customer, and the invoice shows Paid, Partially paid with the balance, or Unpaid. The system won't let you record more than the balance.

A decision is final — a quote can't be flipped from approved to declined later, so record it only once the customer has actually said the word. A quote made before booking shows on the customer's page as Open quote →; when they're ready to schedule, book them via ＋ New Booking as usual.

Deposits, partial payments & receipts

An invoice can be paid in as many pieces as the customer needs — a deposit when the part is ordered, the rest on completion. Every piece is a line in the invoice's payment history, with the date, method, reference, and its RCT- receipt number. Each line has Print receipt (a receipt with your logo showing amount received, paid to date, and balance remaining — it reads exactly as it did when issued) and Email receipt to send it again. The final receipt says paid in full. The Invoices menu shows Total, Paid, and Balance per invoice, and the outstanding figure at the top is only what's actually still owed.

Recorded something wrong, or giving money back? Tap Record refund and enter the amount. Nothing in the payment history is ever edited or deleted — a refund is its own line with its own receipt, so the trail always adds up. A refund can't exceed what was received.

Changing a quote after approval — change orders

Found more work once you're in there? On an approved quote, tap Create change order. The approved lines are copied in; add or edit, then Save change order and Email change order to customer. A yellow banner reminds you the approved quote stands until they answer. Record their answer with Customer approved change — the invoice total updates and the balance moves with it (anything already paid stays credited) — or Customer declined change, which leaves the approved quote and invoice exactly as they were.

Two guardrails: the system refuses to approve a change order that would bring the total below what the customer has already paid (record a refund of the difference first), and once a job is paid in full and completed, additional work is a new job rather than a change order. A declined quote is final — its $80 diagnostic invoice stands, and new work also means a new job.

Finding an invoice later

Three ways, whenever you need to re-print, re-send, or check payment: the Invoices menu lists every invoice (filter by unpaid / partially paid / paid, with the outstanding balance total); the search box jumps straight to an INV-number; or open the customer's page — their Jobs & invoices section links every job and invoice they've ever had, with the balance still due on any partially paid one. Opening any invoice gives you Print / Save PDF, Email invoice to customer (the email now shows paid to date and balance due), and the full payment history with its receipts.

Chapter 10

The warranty

Completing a job records a 2-month labor warranty on the specific repair performed — what was fixed, the part or area, the dates, and who did the work.

When that customer contacts you again while a warranty is active, their request shows the Possible warranty callback banner with the covered repair listed. The system flags — you decide. If the oven you fixed for not heating now has a broken light, that's a new job, not a callback. The banner just makes sure you're never surprised.

Chapter 11

Your team

Manager → Team. When you hire:

  1. Add Technician — name, mobile, email, a lane color for the calendar, and whether they take emergency calls.
  2. Skills — tick exactly what they can fix. This is not decoration: the scheduler will never offer them a job they lack the skill for. An appliance-only tech will never be suggested for an AC repair.
  3. Weekly schedule — their days and hours. These become the only times customers can book them.
  4. Preferred zones — optional; nudges the scheduler to keep them in their part of town.
  5. Create login — shows a temporary password once. Text it to them; they sign in at Team Sign-in and change it via the password link on their Today screen.
  6. Time off — add vacation or sick days here; those days vanish from booking instantly.
Never delete — deactivate. When someone leaves, use Deactivate. Their history stays intact, they disappear from all new scheduling, and any future jobs still on their calendar get flagged on your Today screen for reassignment.

Chapter 12

Settings — the knobs that tune the brain

  • Business — your phone, email, the diagnostic fee amount, warranty length. Change the fee here and the website, booking form, and invoices all follow.
  • Scheduling — how far ahead customers can book, slot granularity, travel buffer, same-day cutoff time, and the recommendation weights: sliders for how much the scheduler values staying in one zone, short drives, balanced workloads, and customer preference. The defaults are sensible; nudge them only if recommendations consistently feel off.
  • Services — what you offer and how long each typically takes. Duration matters: it's how the scheduler knows what fits where.
  • Service Zones — your map of Houston. Add or remove ZIP codes per zone, or tap Draw boundaries → and draw each zone directly on the map. Drawn boundaries take priority over ZIP lists when placing customers.
  • Password — change yours.

Chapter 15

If something looks wrong

  • "The calendar looks empty." Check the date at the top — you may be looking at a quiet day. Sundays are always empty: you're closed, and customers can't book them.
  • "A customer says the site offered no times." Usually every qualified technician is genuinely full (or on time off) for the visible window. Their request still arrives in Requests — schedule it manually or call them.
  • "An address wouldn't validate." The customer is asked to double-check it; addresses outside your zones get a message to call you rather than a rejection. Nothing is lost.
  • "It says the address check is temporarily unavailable." The outside address service is down. Nothing was saved, so keep what you typed on the page and press Save again in a few minutes. Customers on the website see the same message with your phone number, so they can call you to book. If it lasts more than an hour, contact your developer.
  • "I can't put two jobs on one tech at the same time." Correct — that's the system protecting you. Move one job, or give it to someone else.
  • "I forgot my password." Team Sign-in → Forgot password → check your email.
  • Something genuinely broken? Contact your developer (Plaidware Solutions). Every action in the system is recorded, so problems can always be traced.
The philosophy, one last time: the system finds the exact point — the right technician, with the right skill, at the right time, with the least driving — and always shows you why. It recommends; you decide. Every hour it saves you on dispatch is an hour on the tools or with your family.
